# Shard C — Independent discovery

## Findings

- Compared the assigned 4 WON and 3 LOST timelines; labels came from the separate shard plan.
- Technical cooperation, verbal price acceptance, competitor comparison, and scheduled follow-up appear in both outcomes.
- The clearest exploratory contrast is an actionable internal approval path: two LOST cases name an approver but lack access or timing. WON cases also require internal approval, so this needs cross-shard checking.
- Live proof or demonstration is a plausible process feature. In one LOST case the customer sent samples elsewhere after this seller could not demonstrate the line.

## Counterexamples and risks

- Price acceptance, competitor comparison, detailed technical input, and agreed follow-ups all occur in LOST deals; none is a standalone success signal here.
- A named decision-maker is insufficient: LOST deal 18731 has a named director but no review timing.
- Co-design, criteria clarity, and technical-input completeness may be duplicates for the critic to merge.
- Worklog timestamps are approximate (date_only/year_inferred); prefer direct email/transcript evidence to resolve disputes.
- No amount, source, pipeline, duration, call/message count, token volume, final stage, or post-outcome fact is proposed as a semantic feature.
